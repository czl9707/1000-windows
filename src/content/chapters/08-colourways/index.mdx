## Why the wall takes colour

A thousand windows in one ink read as one building, and the street dies. Colour enters
where a hand would put it: the curtain cloth, the awning canvas, the sill ledge, the
window-box wood, the frame's painted face. The drawing stays ink — the line work, the
dots, the sill brackets; the wall stays paper; the glass keeps its whisper wash. The
rule that held since [the layers](/docs/the-layers) still holds: drawing code names
roles, and only `src/styling/` decides colour.

## Each surface rolls its own

Each coloured surface rolls its own colourway off the window seed, on its own stream —
never the [dna](/docs/curtains) or shell streams, so retuning a colour rule never
re-randomizes a tenant. A colourway is a finished colour, picked whole from one
weighted list, and the list carries the rarity: the quiet pale families dominate the
weights; the louder ones are rare rows of the same list, no separate gate to fall
through. Two gates were the reference's habit; one list with honest weights draws the
same street with less machinery, and merging or splitting colours later is a table
edit.

The frame face, the awning canvas, the sill ledge, and the box wood each roll for
themselves, and the curtain's three constructions roll together as one cloth — one
curtain per window, one cloth colour. So a house's surfaces mostly differ, each wearing
its own colour; when two rolls land on the same family the house reads as painted by
one careful owner, and that is chance, not policy. The tint is tenant identity, not
state: it never changes with the page theme.

## Roles apply their own finish

The colourway row is the colour; a role applies only the finish its construction
needs. The cloth washes translucent, the painted faces go down opaque — they exist to
hide the wall and the layers behind them, so anything less lets lines ghost through. A
chroma cap guards each role's saturation ceiling, and a hue jitter rolled with every
colourway keeps neighbours from matching exactly — the pigment mixed fresh at every
house. The awning wears its one colour at two strengths — the fabric's cells
alternate full paint and laid-thin, stripe by stripe — so the stripes carry the
strength where a second colour would only add noise.

## Night

The dark theme is not an inverted drawing and not a second palette; it is the same
street sunk into dark paper. Every coloured surface mixes toward the ground — the
same recipe for every surface — so the hue you saw at noon is still the hue at
midnight, quieter. The mix lives inside the palette resolution: night is a property
of a colour, never a layer drawn over the wall, and nothing about the scene changes
but the colours themselves. The ground itself decides: a paper at half lightness is
night.

## The watercolour pass

A wash at whisper alpha reads as a flat tint. Each cloth wash resolves with its
pooling echo folded in — a lighter pass at half strength, composited into the one
fill styling hands the pen — so the pigment pools and the fill reads as a wash that
dried. Opaque paint carries no echo: paint does not pool. The rim pass — edge
darkening, the real watercolour's calling card — waits with the press layer, where
the print look lives.

## The rolls

One fork, one roll: family, then the jitter band around its hue. A bare window never
reads the tint — the roll still happens, a silently discarded throw, and the stream
stays append-only for the roles that arrive later. What a role may never do is draw in
a guessed colour: a role with no registered resolution crashes at the resolution site.
