## Why the street goes dark

A street where every window burns at the same brightness reads as one office block. Light
enters as a tenant trait, off the [window seed](/docs/randomness) like everything else a
house owns: whether the lamp is on, what warmth it carries, where in the room it stands
and which way it leans. The roll is identity, never state — the same house is lit on
every visit — and most rooms sit dark, so a lit window stays an event on the street.
Every room owns a lamp; the gene decides whether it lights at night, never whether it
exists, so the furniture is always there and only the flame changes.

## The night mix gets selective

[The colour chapter](/docs/colourways) sank every coloured surface into the dark ground
by one mix. Light breaks that apart, because darkness and lamplight are different
mechanisms pretending to be one. The cloth now takes no night mix at all — it relies on
the colour behind it, so a lit room glows through the weave and a dark one goes muddy on
its own. The interior keeps its colours too, and the room's background stays the paper
at all hours. What darkens an unlit room is a veil: one darkening wash drawn right
behind the curtain, over everything the room owns. The darkening moved out of the
palette and into the drawing — a layer a lit room simply does not draw — because a
tenant that reads what is behind it cannot also pre-darken for a lamp it does not know
about.

A lit room runs the other way: the lamp's warmth lays over the interior as the ambient,
one quiet wash covering the room, with the aura — the glow whose shape the construction
casts — on top of that. Both pass directly over what is behind them, ink lines included;
light in a room falls on the furniture, and the drawing should say so. The cloth then
reads the whole lit room through its own wash, which is why the curtain needed its night
mix dropped: the light arrives beneath it, and the weave picks it up.

## The lamp's three parts

Every construction carries the same three parts. The structure is a normal object —
furniture paint and ink line, a dark silhouette in both themes. The emitting part is the
structure's brightest piece: the shade, the bulb, the dome — furniture paint whenever
the lamp is off, the lamp's warmth when it burns, in either state drawn with its ink
edges. By day the lamp is that furniture and nothing more: no veil, no glow, the room
readable in whatever light the sky brings. And the aura, whose shape no catalogue
shares: a shade throws its cone down and
a whisper above, a bare bulb radiates its pool, a drum lights a band and spills from
both rims. Six home constructions ship — the floor lamp with its tapered shade, the
arc floor lamp, the drum-shade table lamp, the small bedside lamp, the pendant with
shade, and the bare-bulb pendant: four standing on the floor line, two hanging from
the ceiling line — picked from a wider prototype sheet, with the list open for
appends. Where the lamp stands and which way it leans rolls like any other gene, and
the lean carries into the glow, so a room lights the corner it lives in.

## Night, the whole street

The wall's night is the full vocabulary at once: most rooms veiled, a few wearing their
warmth, the cloth reading whichever room it hangs in. The benches below pin the night

## What may change later

The insider layers land behind the light block — occupants, greenery, everything the
room will own besides its lamp — and they draw into the same sequence, under the veil
and under the aura, no new mechanism. The warmth table appends rows the way the
colourway list does; more constructions append to the catalogue; the aura's strength
and the veil's depth are tuning dials, and the benches above drive them live.
