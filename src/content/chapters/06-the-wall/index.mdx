[The shell](/docs/window-shell) gave every window its construction;
[motion](/docs/motion) pinned the eye and gave depth its drift.
This chapter is the step where the windows stop being specimens and become
a wall: an infinite grid of them, alive under the pointer, pannable in
every direction. The wall is the home page now — the canvas you are
looking at if you came in through the front door.

The demo is the wall at rest: a frame of windows in their stippled,
patched wall, nothing panning. The eye follows the cursor — the pointer
standing in for where this canvas sits relative to the screen's centre,
the same stand-in [motion](/docs/motion) uses; on the home wall
that offset is the canvas's real position. Panning lives on the home
page — drag and let go: the wall glides, decelerates, and settles.
Everywhere it applies the same rule: every visible window redraws every
frame, and the same coordinates always show the same tenant, no matter
how the camera arrived.

## The grid and its scale

The wall is a grid of cells, one window per cell — no vacancies, no
searching for the empty lots. A cell's identity is its integer
coordinates, and its seed is `windowSeed(metaSeed, col, row)`: the
coordinates exist inside that one call and nowhere else, so pan history
cannot reach a tenant even in principle.

The pitch is deliberately sparse — several units of bare wall around
each window box — so every window stands in its own frame of wall and
the neighbours never crowd it. How large a unit lands on screen is not
a constant — it is a rule about counts, decided once and enforced by the
viewport: a minimum view in columns and rows, a cap on the visible cell
count, a floor under each window's rendered size. Where the bounds
conflict, the count bounds yield first: a shrinking viewport drops
columns and rows rather than shrinking tenants into specks, a phone
shows a couple of large windows instead of a crowd of small ones, and
only past the floor does the cap get a word in. The cell's screen size
adjusts to hit the bounds; the bounds are never enforced by cutting the
draw short.

## Culling and the pen lifecycle

Each frame asks one pure question — which cells' window boxes cross the
camera-centred extent, widened by a margin of at least one cell — and the
answer is a list of coordinates:

```ts
export function visibleCells(camera: Camera, extentW: number, extentH: number, margin = 0): Cell[]
```

The pens follow the same rule for both layers of the wall — windows and
texture slots alike: a pen is created when its cell enters the extended
viewport and dropped when it leaves. The pen's internal trace cache dies
with the instance, so nothing outlives the pan history: no cache grows
with distance travelled, and memory stays bounded by what is on screen.

```ts
export function planPens(active: ReadonlySet<string>, wanted: readonly string[]): PenPlan
```

The diff is pure and keyed by canonical `"col,row"` strings — a cell seen
across two frames is two separate objects, so structural identity is
spelled, not referenced.

## The wall between the windows

The bare paper between windows carries the settled texture treatment,
ported from the throwaway prototype: the paper stipple tile across the
whole wall, and ragged illustrative brick-patch islands over it — sparse
overshooting course lines with staggered vertical joints, drawn `ruhig`
(mortar does not tremble), at a whisper of ink. Both are seeded per wall
slot from the meta-seed, so the surface varies region to region, survives
panning exactly as a window does, and re-rolls when the wall shuffles.

## Drag, inertia, rest

Panning moves the world; the eye stays at the screen's centre, exactly as
[motion](/docs/motion) fixed it. A drag translates the camera
under the pointer; a release hands the smoothed drag velocity to the
glide — velocity decaying exponentially under friction, the camera
advancing by the decay's exact integral, so the physics is deterministic
in `(camera, velocity, dt)`:

```ts
export function glide(camera: Camera, velocity: Velocity, dt: number, friction: number = FRICTION)
```

Below the rest threshold a glide snaps still rather than crawling. A
release shorter than a few pixels is a click: it never nudges the wall.
The gestures are part of the wall module too — a pannable surface binds
`dragWall(wall, canvas, unitPx)` once, and the strip above is the same
engine with the gestures left unbound.

## What the wall does not know

A whole frame of wall is one call — `wall.draw(ctx, box, frame, time, colors)` —
the engine entrypoint that owns the meta-seed, the camera, and both layers'
pens; reseed, glide, and the pan gestures go through the same object. Inside
that frame, the wall composites windows through one call —
`drawWindow(ctx, pen, palette, eye)` — and that is the whole contract. It
knows nothing of shells, tenants, curtains, or light. Everything that
happens inside a window lands behind that entry, and the home page holds
nothing but the wiring around the engine: the frame's view and real eye,
and the shuffle seat.
