A wall of a thousand windows cannot give every one a bespoke drawing — the
variety has to come from a small catalogue of frame constructions, rolled
per window from its seed. The shell is everything the building owns:
archetype, pane layout, open state, sill, and the add-ons mounted
over them. Who lives behind the glass is the tenant, and the tenant's
chapters come later; here we decide the frame, and the code pattern that
keeps the frame's vocabulary from trampling the tenant's.

One ratio holds for every window on the wall: a wide rectangle, `1.6 × 1.0`
in unit measure (see [The pen](/docs/the-pen)). The archetypes are frame
constructions inside that box — no arched tops, no portholes. Glass is
the one thing the shell tints: every pane carries a wash of ink
(`Pen.fill`, a flat translucent shape — stroke, dot, and fill are the
pen's three tools), at a strength where it stays under the lines. Where
two panes overlap — a sliding panel riding behind its neighbour — the
washes stack. Everything else sits on paper: the whole box fills in the
paper colour beneath its lines, the sill's face the same way, so the
window reads as a texture-free plane against the textured wall. Every
pane draws its own four border lines, regardlessly,
even where they ride on the outer mullion: the doubled edge is the
hand-set look. A pane edge shared with a neighbouring pane — an inner
splitter — draws lighter than the frame weight, and an open window is
bare paper bounded by those lines, the way a pen drawing has to say it.
The add-ons fill opaque over what they mount over — an awning, a shutter
panel, or a window box hides the wall and the layers behind it.

## The archetypes

Three frame constructions, weighted toward what dominates a real street
of wide openings. The plate pins every layout: the sliders on the first
row, the casements and picture on the second.

<DemoCanvas demo="shell-archetypes" label="Demo canvas — the archetypes and their layouts" />

**Sliding**, weight 4 — panels on two tracks, one behind the other.
First row, from left: `two` (weight 4), `three` (2), `transom` (2) —
the transom pins a fixed picture strip across the head, the sliding row
beneath it. The panels alternate tracks; a sliding panel travels toward
its fixed neighbour and hides behind it — never past the stile, so a
strip of the sliding glass stays visible and the panels never close up
into one. The front track stands a hair off the wall, so the tracks
separate under the parallax.

**Casement**, weight 3 — leaves that swing out of the wall, beside fixed
picture panes. Second row, from left: `pair` (4) — two leaves, each
half the opening, both hinged at the outer jambs; `one-two` (2) — a
narrow leaf on the jamb, a wide fixed pane beside it. The casement
never rolls an undivided single leaf: a swung lone leaf over an
undivided pane would read as a picture window that came open, so an
undivided pane says picture, always.

**Picture**, weight 2 — fixed glass, no divisions, no lattice, never
open. The wash says glass; the absence of divisions says picture.

Layouts are **named kinds picked by one roll**, never assembled from
independent axes: there is no "rows × columns × transom" multiplication,
because independent axes multiply against each other and produce
combinations nobody designed. Each name is a drawing the makers agreed
on.

## The open states

Sliding and casement are operable; picture is fixed glass. The operable
archetypes roll an open state — closed (weight 4), open (2), half-open
(1) — skewed toward closed, like everything else on the street.

<DemoCanvas demo="shell-open" label="Demo canvas — the open states, sliding above casement" />

Top row, the slider *glides*: one panel — the rolled side — travels
toward its fixed neighbour and disappears behind it, stopping while a
strip of its glass still shows past the neighbour's stile — the panel
never reaches the jamb, and the panes never close up into one. On a
three-panel slider only one panel moves; two sliding at once reads as
a smear. Bottom row, the casement *swings*: the leaves turn toward the
viewer, hinged at their jambs. A leaf keeps its true rectangle,
narrowed by the swing's cosine, and its points stand out of the wall at
the swing's depth — the free edge rides `span · sin(swing)` in front of
the wall plane — so the projection and the parallax
([Motion](/docs/motion)) place it where a swung leaf is. The opened
leaf carries its glass with it, and its border draws at the frame
weight; the fixed pane beside it keeps its glass, flat on the wall. The
plate's casements show a closed pair, a half-open pair, and a pair
swinging from both jambs.

## The add-ons

Masses mounted over frame and sill, each its own roll. From left,
top row: the metal `slope` awning — a rigid plane whose ribs the
projection skews — the fabric `canopy`, an arc with a scalloped valance,
and the `louvered` shutters; bottom row: the `paneled` shutters and the
two box counts. The none weights sit behind the plates: awning `none` 4,
shutters `none` 4, box `none` 8 — most windows on a street are bare.

<DemoCanvas demo="shell-addons" label="Demo canvas — the add-on kinds" />

The shutters mount on sliding windows only — they flank a wall of
sliding panes the way they flank a door, never a swung casement — and
are two panels half the opening's width each, hung at its
outer edges and swung half open toward the viewer: swung they cover no
glass, draw narrowed by the swing's cosine, and fill opaque against the
wall behind them. Like the open casement leaf, their points stand out
of the wall at the swing's depth — depth grows with distance from the
hinge, at the swing's tangent — so the panels ride in front of the wall
under the parallax. The awning sorts above the shutters — the roof
stands over them — and the window box hangs over the sill so it reads
as passing behind them, a few tufts over each lip. The awning runs wider
than the window box so it reads as a roof, and it fills opaque — the
fabric hides the frame top and everything behind it. The add-ons may
reach 0.35 beyond the box sideways and 0.4 above it — the wall's window
pitch keeps neighbours from ever touching. The sill's depth and the
awning's reach are constants per kind, never rolls: two same-looking
windows that differ only in ledge depth read as identical at wall
distance, and a seed draw spent on variety nobody can see is a draw
wasted.

## The rolls

The shell rolls on its own stream, `fork(seed, 'shell')`, separate from
the DNA stream the tenant will draw from. The fork is what lets one
vocabulary grow without re-randomizing the other: appending a shell roll
shifts nothing behind the glass, and appending a gene shifts nothing in
the frames. The order is fixed — archetype, then pane layout, then open
state, then slide side (when a slider rolls), then the measured
rolls (thickness jitter, face width), then sill and the three add-on
masses — and new rolls append at the end, never between. Shutters roll
on every window and mount on sliders only: every window consumes the
same roll sequence shape, whatever it draws.

After the categorical rolls, two measured rolls: the frame thickness
jitters ±15% around its archetype constant, and the frame face — the
run between the outer and inner line — rolls between `0.05` and `0.09`.
That is the whole measured budget.

```ts
function expandShell(seed: number): ShellPlan
```

`expandShell` is the only place that rolls. The drawers are pure emitters
(`drawSliding`, `drawCasement`, `drawPicture`, `drawSill`,
`drawAwning`, `drawShutters`, `drawWindowBox`): each turns a plan into
[marks](/docs/the-layers), and `drawWindow(ctx, pen, palette)` collects,
sorts by depth, projects, and lets the pen draw. The pen carries the
window's seed — the plan expands from `pen.seed`, so the shell and the
tremor can never disagree. The trace names follow
[the pen](/docs/the-pen): bare ids such as `slide-0-top`, `leaf-1-left`,
`awning-rib-2`, warped off the window seed, unique within a window — a
test holds every drawer to it.

## What may change later

The catalogue is data, and data gets edited: weights may change,
archetypes, layouts, and add-on kinds may be inserted. What may never
change is the shape of the promise: a window seed's roll sequence is a
pure function of the seed, never of pan position or render order.
Because the meta-seed is random per page load, a catalogue edit
re-dresses the whole street anyway — nobody's window survives a version
bump, so nobody's window is asked to.
